Overview

The SecAct platform helps researchers investigate intercellular signaling mediated by secreted proteins (e.g., cytokines, growth factors, and secreted enzymes). In addition, users can query the spatial landscape of any human gene across a large collection of tumor spatial transcriptomics (ST) datasets.

Tumor ST Atlas

Explore the spatial landscape of any gene across 1,258 tumor ST samples and compare spatial variability, cell types, and pathways.



Explore

SecAct Database

Browse spatial relationships between secreted proteins and their potential signaling targets or mediators across the Tumor ST Atlas.



Browse

SecAct R Package

Run SecAct online to infer secreted protein activity from your own gene expression data, or install it from GitHub for local analysis.



Run


Input a gene symbol:

  • Spatial Variability
  • Spatial Co-Variability
  • Cell Type
  • Hallmark
  • KEGG Pathway
  • GO Biological Process

Frequently Asked Questions

                

                

                  1. How are spatial variability, cell type fractions, and hallmark/pathway/biological process gene set scores inferred in the "Explore ST Atlas" tab?
                

                

                  A: These analyses were computed using our previously developed R package SpaCET (
                  https://github.com/data2intelligence/SpaCET
                  ).
                

                

                

                  2. What does Moran’s I represent?
                

                
A: Moran’s I is a measure of spatial autocorrelation, ranging from −1 to 1.
                A higher positive value indicates stronger spatial clustering,
                a value near 0 suggests a random spatial pattern,
                and a negative value indicates spatial dispersion.
                It is commonly used to quantify the spatial variability of individual genes.

                

                  In addition, bivariate Moran’s I is used to assess the spatial co-variability of a pair of genes.
                A higher positive value indicates stronger spatial co-localization or co-variability,
                whereas a negative value suggests spatial mutual exclusivity between the two genes.
                More details about the equation can be found
                  here
                  .
                

                

                

                  3. What are the normalized Moran’s I values in the "Browse SecActDB" tab?
                

                
A: In “Explore ST Atlas”, the displayed Moran’s I values are raw values.
                In contrast, “Browse SecActDB” shows normalized Moran’s I values from our final secreted protein signatures used in SecAct activity inference model.
                Therefore, the values for the same gene pair may differ slightly between the two tabs.

                
During secreted protein signature construction, we normalized bivariate Moran’s I values across genes to make them comparable across spatial transcriptomics (ST) profiles using two steps.
                (1) Within each ST sample, Moran’s I values for all secreted proteins were mean-centered before constructing composite signatures.
                (2) When combining signatures for the same secreted protein across ST samples, low-confidence signatures were removed.

                

                

                  4. Why can't I find my secreted protein or gene of interest in "Browse SecActDB"?
                

                

                  A: The Browse SecActDB module includes only high-confidence relationships between secreted proteins and their downstream targets identified after the filtering steps described in the SecAct 
                  paper
                  . If your secreted protein or gene of interest is not available, you can use the "Explore ST Atlas" module to examine its spatial expression patterns across tumor spatial transcriptomics datasets.
                

                

                

                  5. Why do I not see a boxplot or spatial landscape for a cancer type in "Explore ST Atlas"?
                

                
A: Click a bar or heatmap tile to display the corresponding boxplot;
                then click a dot in the boxplot to visualize the spatial landscape of that feature.

                

                

                  6. How to infer secreted protein activities for large-scale datasets?
                

                

                  A: Install R package SecAct (
                  https://github.com/data2intelligence/SecAct
                  ) locally and run it on your own data.
                The package supports large-scale analyses and provides a variety of downstream analysis and visualization modules.
                

                

                

              

            

          

          

            

              

                
Data Availability

                  https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17664022
                  ).
